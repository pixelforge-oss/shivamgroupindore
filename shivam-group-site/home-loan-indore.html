How Much Home Loan Can You Get?

Lenders don't just look at your gross salary — they look at how much of it is already committed to other EMIs and expenses (often called your Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio, or FOIR). Many lenders try to keep your total EMIs, including the new home loan, within roughly 40–50% of your net monthly income, though the exact figure varies by lender, your age and the tenure offered.

Use our EMI calculator to see how the loan amount, interest rate and tenure affect your monthly payment before you approach a lender.

Fixed vs Floating Interest Rate

A floating rate moves with the lender's benchmark, so your EMI can rise or fall over the loan's life — this is how most home loans in India are structured today. A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period but is usually priced a little higher.

One thing worth knowing: floating-rate home loans taken by individuals for personal use currently carry no foreclosure or prepayment penalty under RBI rules, which makes it easier to prepay early or switch lenders later if you find a better deal.

Home Loan FAQs for Indore Buyers

Straight answers to the questions we actually get asked in Indore.

What documents do I need for a home loan in Indore?

Most lenders ask for the same core set: identity and address proof (Aadhaar and PAN), recent photographs, income proof (salary slips and Form 16 for salaried applicants, or ITRs and bank statements for the self-employed), your last six months' bank statements, and the property's title and sale documents. Self-employed applicants may also need GST returns or business registration proof. The exact list can vary slightly by lender.

How much home loan can I get based on my salary?

Lenders look at how much of your income is already committed to other EMIs (your Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio). Many try to keep your total EMIs within roughly 40–50% of your net monthly income, though this varies by lender, your age and the tenure. Our EMI calculator can help you see how amount, rate and tenure interact before you apply.

Can I get a home loan for a resale flat or an older property?

Yes, most banks finance resale properties, but they also look at the building's age and remaining structural life, since the loan tenure is usually capped so it ends before the property crosses a certain age. Clear title documents and a proper ownership chain matter more for resale purchases, so it helps to have these ready in advance.

What if my building's construction isn't fully approved by the IDA or Nagar Nigam?

Nationalized banks generally want construction to match the sanctioned map, and significant deviations can lead to rejection or a reduced loan amount. If there's a gap between your building's actual construction and its approved plan, it's worth getting this checked before you apply — some lenders take a case-by-case view based on the property's documented value. We can help you understand where a specific property stands before you commit to one lender.

Should I choose a fixed or a floating interest rate?

A floating rate moves with the lender's benchmark rate over time, while a fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period (most home loans in India today are floating). Floating-rate home loans for personal use also currently carry no foreclosure or prepayment penalty under RBI rules, which makes switching lenders later easier if you find a better rate.

Can self-employed professionals or business owners in Indore get a home loan?

Yes. Self-employed applicants are assessed on income tax returns (usually the last 2–3 years), business continuity and bank statement patterns rather than a salary slip. It takes a bit more documentation, but it's a well-served category with most lenders, including for business owners around Indore's trading and industrial areas.
